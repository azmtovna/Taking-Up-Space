Cold open

“When I say that children are a burden on society, I really mean it.”Gauhar, mother of four · translated from Russian

Taking Up Space

A documentary multimedia project about Gauhar, a mother of four, and the work of making room for a family after divorce.

The question. What does it cost a mother raising four children alone to be seen only as “a woman with kids” — and who is expected to pay that cost?

By Aida Azamatkyzy

She is not describing regret about her children. She is describing the pressure of caring for them while feeling that their presence requires an apology. Her account begins with a provocative phrase; the story behind it is about work, time and support.

The door

The author recalls accompanying Gauhar and three children when a taxi driver asked them to cancel their booking, citing the absence of child seats. They ordered a business-class car and paid more to complete the journey.

The experience raises a practical question: how does a parent arrange an affordable journey with several children? It does not, by itself, establish hostility towards children. A driver's stated safety concern is different from an assumption about the driver's motives.

The day

Gauhar says she trained as a pharmacist. According to information provided by the author, she has worked as a hospital nurse since 2025, on a part-time arrangement, finishing at 3 p.m. She is looking for better-paid work.

  1. In the morning she takes the youngest to kindergarten and the older ones to school, then goes to work.
  2. Her parents collect the youngest from kindergarten, take them home and wait for her.
  3. She walks to the school to pick up the older children, goes home and cooks.

“I have no money for a nanny, sorry.”

Photo supplied by Gauhar, used with her consent.

She lives with her four children, without another adult in the household. That is not the same as having no help. Her parents’ contribution belongs in the story of how her working day becomes possible.

Four doors

Gauhar recalls encounters in a clinic, a shop, job interviews and the rental market. The clinic and shopping experiences belong to an earlier period, when the children were younger. Choose an account to read.

The changed answer

Gauhar says that after answering questions about her family honestly in early job interviews, she began giving a different answer: she said she had a husband and a stable household income.

“I started saying that yes, I have a husband, we have a steady income… because otherwise they couldn’t accept me.”

She is employed now and wants better-paid work. The question is not simply whether a job exists, but how a different job would fit the arrangements that allow her to work and care for four children.

The work that continues at home

Gauhar’s experience sits within a measurable national difference in the time women and men spend running a household.

These are population averages, not figures for divorced mothers, mothers of four, or Gauhar herself. They do not measure public attitudes or establish discrimination.

A wider care gap

In 2023, an estimated 708 million women and 40 million men aged 15 and above were outside the labour force because of care responsibilities, according to International Labour Organization estimates drawing on data from 125 countries.

Gauhar is employed. She is not an example of being outside the labour force. The global figures provide a wider frame for understanding why the organisation of care matters to employment, without describing her individual circumstances.

These historical accounts do not establish how common Gauhar’s experience is. Their details and policy amounts should not be treated as present-day conditions.

The apology

“The hardest part isn’t the physical tiredness. It’s the constant feeling that you must apologize to everyone just because your children exist and take up space in society.”

“I don’t need pity. But maybe think: perhaps she has no husband. Perhaps he died. I’m not enjoying this either.”

“Of course I love my children. Do I regret having them? No.”

Gauhar is already working and continues to seek better pay. The divorce is in the past; organising income and care remains a task in the present. The account ends with that search still open, not with a manufactured resolution.

About this project

Reporting, translation and editing by Aida Azamatkyzy.

Reporting, consent and sources

The project draws on a Russian-language interview with Gauhar, translated into English, the author’s recollection of a taxi encounter she attended, family photographs and follow-up information supplied by the author. Gauhar is a relative of the author. Quotations and accounts of earlier events are distinguished from the present-day routine.

The author has supplied the photographs for use in the project. Before public release, the scope of Gauhar’s informed consent, photo authorship and any identifying information must be confirmed. Children’s photographs are not needed to substantiate the reporting and are not displayed in this revision.

The national and global statistics describe different populations and are not measurements of Gauhar’s household. The additional profiles are credited to their original publications.
